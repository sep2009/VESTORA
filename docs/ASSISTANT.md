# Stylist assistant (`/assistant`, the "Ask the stylist" button)

A conversational stylist that builds outfits, finds matches, searches by budget and shows where a department is in the 3D mall. **It works fully without any AI model**: the built-in engine is rule-based and is always labelled "Rule-based stylist · no AI model connected". A language model is an optional, validated add-on, and **no real model is integrated yet**.

## What it does
- **Chat UI** (`components/assistant/`): premium conversation with suggested questions (the eight scenarios from the brief), quick-action chips after each answer, product cards, outfit cards, loading indicator, history that survives reloads and page changes, "Clear conversation", and a "What I can use" panel. It is on the main navigation (`Stylist`), and a floating **Ask the stylist** button opens the same chat in a side panel on shopping pages (shop, product, mall, cart, favorites, Outfit Studio, For you …). On a product page it reads "Ask about this product".
- **Understanding** (`modules/assistant/core.ts`, deterministic): occasion (birthday, party, formal event, work, university, sport, weekend, travel), season, style, formality, budget ("under $200", "$300 max"), colors to include or avoid, product types, a named product or "this jacket", a department, and follow-ups ("different outfits", "cheaper", "dressier", "more casual", "add a bag"). It shows what it understood ("I understood: a birthday party · under $300").
- **Follow-up questions**: "What is the outfit for?" when nothing is known; "What's your total budget?" when you say "within my budget" without a number (or uses your saved per-item budget if you allow your style profile); "Which product should I match?" with choices. Answers can be typed or tapped. Refinements keep the rest of the request; a new topic starts fresh (keeping budget and avoided colors).
- **Outfits**: up to three distinct outfits from recipes per situation (formal, party, work, university, sport, winter, summer, mild, minimalist, casual). Each piece is chosen from in-stock catalog products by color compatibility, style, occasion and your allowed profile (Outfit Studio and style-engine rules). Layers follow body slots (a dress replaces top and bottoms). Summer excludes coats and hoodies, winter requires outerwear, minimalist favors neutral palettes.
- **Each outfit shows**: name, occasion, product images, names, brands, prices (sale price when on sale), the **total**, available sizes and colors (only in-stock ones), the color combination and a color story, a compatibility score, and a styling explanation built from the pieces' real data (no invented fabrics or fit claims).
- **Per outfit actions**: replace any piece (compatible in-stock alternatives within what is left of the budget), choose color and size, open each product, **preview on the avatar** (Outfit Studio's generic mannequin; preview mode, not a fitting), **save** to Outfit Studio's saved outfits (without touching the outfit being edited), open in Outfit Studio, **add available items to the cart** (requires an in-stock size/color; incomplete items are named and skipped).
- **Budget**: a total budget is enforced by swapping the dearest pieces for cheaper compatible ones, then dropping optional extras, never by inventing prices. If nothing fits, it says so and gives the lowest total it could build for that request. "Cheaper" sets the budget to 80% of the last total.
- **Find shoes matching this jacket**: matching in-stock pieces for the slot you name, each with why it matches, plus outfits built around that product. A sold-out product is not built around ("sold out right now").

## Store navigation (department → real place in the mall)
When a department is recommended (you ask for one, or an outfit's pieces come from it) the answer includes a **department card** with a floor plan drawn from the mall's own layout data (`modules/showroom/layout.ts`), the department highlighted, a dashed route from the entrance, its position ("in the north-east part of the mall"), step-by-step directions from the entrance through the real adjoining rooms, its neighbors, and a few matching products. Actions:
- On a page other than the mall: **Explore this department** (opens the mall at that department via the existing `/?dept=` link) and Browse in the shop.
- Inside the mall: **Show on the minimap** (outlines the room on the mall's minimap; the camera does not move) and **Take me there** (flies the camera to that room, only on request).
The stylist never moves the camera, opens a page or changes the minimap by itself.

## Context, consent and privacy
Used only if you allow it in "What I can use": the page you opened it from (product or department; on by default because you opened it there), your style quiz profile, your cart, your saved outfits (all off by default). The answer says which were used. Rules run on our server; **message text is not stored or logged** and the conversation lives only on your device (cleared with "Clear conversation"). Style profile, cart and saved outfits are never sent to a model provider: the provider receives only your message, the last few messages and a non-identifying summary of what was understood (occasion, season, budget).

## AI backend (optional) and why the model cannot invent products
`server/assistant/` implements the secure service:
- `providers/provider.ts`: the contract. One adapter file per provider; **none exists yet** (do not guess an API; see below). `providers/index.ts` is an empty registry chosen only by `ASSISTANT_PROVIDER`. `providers/test-provider.ts` is a scripted development provider that is **not an AI model**, labelled "Scripted test model (not AI)" everywhere, used to test the pipeline (`ASSISTANT_PROVIDER=test`, `ALLOW_TEST_ASSISTANT=1` in production).
- **Tools** (`chat.ts`, `ToolApi`): `searchProducts`, `getProduct`, `buildOutfits`, `matchesFor`, `departmentInfo`, all backed by the product database and returning only in-stock items. The model works through these and returns a **plan** of product ids.
- **Validation** (`validatePlan` in `core.ts`): every product id must exist, be in stock, share the catalog currency, fit together in body slots and respect the budget; unknown ids, unknown departments, sold-out items, conflicting pieces and over-budget outfits are dropped. **Prices, stock, sizes and links are never taken from the model**: cards are rebuilt from the database. The model's text may not contain links or any money amount that is not exactly a verified price or total; otherwise the text is replaced by the rule-based text and a notice says so. If nothing verifiable remains, the answer falls back to rules with a notice.
- **Failure handling**: unreachable, timeout (`ASSISTANT_TIMEOUT_MS`), unusable reply, or unverifiable output → a clear message ("The AI service is unavailable right now"), the rule-based suggestions marked as such, links to browse the shop with filters, and the conversation kept (a failed send can be retried). A successful AI response is never fabricated; the source line under every answer says "Rule-based suggestions" or names the provider.
- Questions that need a clarifying answer, and every request when no provider is configured, are answered by the rules and never sent to a provider.

### Adding a real model later
1. Choose a provider and read its official documentation (authentication, how tool or function calling works, structured output, limits, pricing, data retention and training terms, regions).
2. Write `providers/<name>.ts` implementing `AssistantProvider.respond`: give the model the `ToolApi` functions as tools, instruct it to use only ids from tool results and to put no prices, links or stock in its text, and return `{ reply, outfits, products, department }`. Fill `privacyNote` from the provider's own terms (shown to the shopper).
3. Register it in `providers/index.ts`, keep credentials in server-only environment variables, set `ASSISTANT_PROVIDER`.
4. Review the provider's terms against the privacy policy. Run `node scripts/test-assistant.mjs` and add provider-specific failure cases.

## Not done / limits
- **No real language model is connected.** What is shown as "the stylist" is rules over the catalog. It understands a defined set of phrasings (English only) and answers "I didn't catch that" with options otherwise; it is not open-ended conversation. A real model would improve free-form understanding but is not needed for any recommendation here.
- Occasion, season and style knowledge are general rules about garment types and this catalog's departments, not tagged data; there is no knowledge of weather, dress codes or trends.
- Outfit compatibility percentages are the outfit engine's own scores (color, style, occasion), not a measure of how an outfit looks on a person. The avatar preview is generic.
- The conversation is per device (not synced to accounts). Rate limits are in memory.
- Not tested: screen readers on the chat log, a real touch device, languages other than English.
